World Values Survey – Synthetic Data Explorer

This page presents an exploratory analysis of a synthetic (simulated) dataset modelled on the World Values Survey, Wave 7 (Haerpfer, C., Inglehart, R., Moreno, A., Welzel, C., Kizilova, K., Diez-Medrano, J., Lagos, M., Norris, P., Ponarin, E., & Puranen, B. (eds.), 2022. World Values Survey: Round Seven – Country-Pooled Datafile. Madrid & Vienna: JD Systems Institute & WVSA Secretariat).

The rows are entirely fabricated to resemble real WVS distributions and relationships — no actual respondent data is used. Any patterns shown are illustrative only.

Countries included: China, India, Kazakhstan, Singapore, and Turkey (8,495 respondents after cleaning; 9,329 original rows).

1. Cultural Map — Emancipative vs Secular Values

Each point is a country average (Inglehart–Welzel style).

China scores highest on secular values and Singapore leads on emancipative values, while India shows the lowest secular-values score. The five countries span a wide range on the Inglehart–Welzel cultural map.

2. Value Fingerprints — Radar Chart

Six measures normalised to 0–1. One line per country.

Countries differ most sharply on importance of god and interpersonal trust, while financial satisfaction shows the smallest gap across the five countries.

3. Individual Values — China vs India

~300 randomly sampled respondents per country; larger outlined points mark the country average.

Despite clearly different averages, individual responses from China and India overlap substantially — many respondents from both countries share similar value profiles.
